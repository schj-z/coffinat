Lower is better for sleep, and caffeine can disrupt it hours after the last drink: in one controlled study a 400 mg dose still measurably reduced sleep taken 6 h before bed, and EFSA notes ~100 mg near bedtime can affect some adults. How this works

Each drink is modelled with a one-compartment pharmacokinetic (Bateman) model. It uses a first-order absorption rate chosen to give a typical peak around 30–60 minutes after a drink (so your level rises gradually, it does not jump), then eliminates with a half-life of about 5 hours. The result is an estimate in mg/L that approximates the plasma / serum concentrations reported in the pharmacokinetic literature — it is not a whole-blood measurement, and "blood caffeine" on this page is friendly shorthand for that estimate. The scale comes from a volume of distribution of ≈0.6 L/kg × your body mass. Each day also includes the residual caffeine carried over from previous days. Constants are population averages; your own metabolism may differ substantially.

The plausible range (shaded band)

The solid line is a single best estimate; nobody is exactly average, so the shaded band shows how uncertain it is. It is built by re-running the model across a small set of plausible values for the things we cannot know exactly — your volume of distribution, your elimination half-life, how fast you absorb caffeine, and how much caffeine each drink really held — and shading between the lowest and highest resulting curves. It is a plausible range, not a statistically calibrated confidence interval. It widens later in the day, because small differences in half-life compound as caffeine is cleared, and a home-brewed coffee widens it more than a known, measured caffeine amount does. A drink you're only planning (the forecast) gets the same band around its own line.

Your privacy

Your entries stay on your device. Your body mass, drinks, times, sleep goal and any custom product names are computed in your browser and saved only in its localStorage. The app itself transmits none of it: beyond downloading its own files, it makes no network requests and uses no cookies, analytics or trackers. Clearing your browser storage deletes your entries; there is no account, and nothing you type is stored on a server.

One honest caveat: loading any website — including this one — means contacting whatever server hosts it (for example GitHub Pages). Like every web page, that host sees standard request metadata such as your IP address, browser user-agent and which files you fetched, and may keep server logs. That is outside this project's control and separate from anything you enter here; Coffinat adds no collection of its own on top of it.

Limitations

This is an educational model, not a measurement. Every constant is a population average; real caffeine metabolism varies widely with genetics, smoking, pregnancy, medication, liver function and more, and the effect bands depend heavily on tolerance. The home-brew figures are rough estimates that ignore grind, agitation and water chemistry. The model shows an estimate; it does not tell you an amount that is safe to drink. Not medical advice — do not use it for medical or dosing decisions.

At very high exposures the model breaks down. Above the toxic range (≈15 mg/L) caffeine elimination can become nonlinear and substantially slower than this constant-half-life model assumes, so toxic-range estimates should not be used to predict clearance or recovery time — the app marks them unreliable and shows a warning rather than a false-precise "back below X by HH:MM". If your central estimate stays below the range but the plausible band still reaches into it, you'll see a gentler caution instead. A real caffeine overdose is a medical emergency.

Sources

All constants are population averages from published sources:

  • Caffeine pharmacokinetics — StatPearls, NIH (NBK519490); Alsabri et al., J. Caffeine Res. 2018; EFSA caffeine assessment.
  • Daily limits (400 mg/day; 200 mg single dose) — EFSA Scientific Opinion on caffeine (2015).
  • Caffeine and sleep timing — Drake et al., J. Clin. Sleep Med. 2013 (a 400 mg dose disrupted sleep even taken 6 h before bed); EFSA additionally notes ~100 mg near bedtime can affect sleep in some adults.
  • Toxicity bands — Caffeine Toxicity, StatPearls (NBK532910) (toxicity from ≈15 mg/L, ≈80–100 mg/L potentially lethal). The lower "effect" bands on the ladder are illustrative — subjective effects map poorly onto blood levels — and are not taken from this source.
  • Brew extraction (bean pool, method efficiency) — practical brewing references, not analytical studies: Arabica vs Robusta & by method; how brew methods affect caffeine. The per-gram pools and method efficiencies are representative values, and the extraction-over-time curve is a modelling assumption — not measurements from these pages.
  • What's evidence-based vs. chosen. Well-supported: the volume of distribution, average half-life, near-complete absorption, the EFSA intake figures and the high-concentration toxicity thresholds. Deliberately simplified: the one-compartment model and its single absorption rate. Heuristic (documented estimates, not measured constants): the subjective tolerance factors and every home-brew parameter. The shaded uncertainty band is a non-probabilistic scenario range over plausible physiology and drink content — a spread, not a probability.
